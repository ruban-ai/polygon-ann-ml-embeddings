\begin{abstract}
Similarity search over polygon datasets relies on Weighted Jaccard (WJ) to
capture area-weighted shape overlap. ShapeToVec~\cite{shape2vec} encodes each
polygon as an 18{,}219-dimensional probability-simplex vector indexed with
HNSW~\cite{hnsw20} in nmslib's \texttt{WeightedJaccard} space~\cite{shape2vec},
but the dimensionality is costly to store and search. We learn a compact,
WJ-native simplex embedding (up to $71\times$ smaller). A contrastive route
(triplet, reconstruction, InfoNCE) is strong on a 10k subset but, at the full
187k scale, is \emph{no better than an untrained random projection}: these are
separation proxies that do not preserve the WJ ranking recall depends on, and the
loss even damages the deployed output layer. We instead \emph{distill the metric}
--- regressing the embedding's WJ onto the true WJ aligns the objective with the
retrieval metric. A single Matryoshka~\cite{matryoshka22} encoder then surpasses
random projection at \emph{both} near- and wide-net recall at both scales: 10k
Recall@50 $=0.93$ (above brute-force ICWS) at 14.5k QPS, and at 187k Stage-1
Recall@500 of 0.890 vs.\ 0.722 and 0.966 vs.\ 0.927 after reranking at
$\sim$2$\times$ throughput, on all 46{,}754 held-out queries --- already at 256 dimensions.
\end{abstract}

%% ---------------- 1. Introduction ----------------
\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
Geospatial applications increasingly search large polygon datasets --- parks,
water bodies, parcels --- for similar shapes, where Weighted Jaccard (WJ)
\begin{equation}
  WJ(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}) = \frac{\sum_i \min(a_i,b_i)}{\sum_i \max(a_i,b_i)},
  \quad \mathbf{a},\mathbf{b} \ge \mathbf{0},
  \label{eq:wj}
\end{equation}
is the standard area-weighted overlap metric. The core difficulty is
\emph{extreme area variability}: polygon areas span orders of magnitude, so a
uniform grid either explodes in dimension or collapses small shapes into
near-empty vectors. ShapeToVec~\cite{shape2vec} resolves this with an adaptive
\emph{quadtree} grid, encoding each polygon as an 18{,}219-d probability-simplex
vector whose WJ preserves overlap across scales; indexed with HNSW~\cite{hnsw20}
in nmslib's \texttt{WeightedJaccard} space~\cite{shape2vec} it reaches $97\%$ R@50
on up to 1.7M polygons. But such large vectors are costly to store and search;
compressing them while preserving WJ structure is the goal of this work.

Existing tools do not fit. WJ sketches (ICWS~\cite{ioffe10},
ProbMinHash~\cite{probminhash20}, PolyMinHash~\cite{polyminhash25}) output integer
codes needing brute-force or LSH-band search; learned hashing and metric
learning~\cite{hashsurvey14,deephashsurvey22} target Hamming or cosine spaces ---
none produce WJ-compatible embeddings. Our contributions:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=1pt]
  \item A \textbf{WJ-native simplex embedding}: ReLU\,+\,$\ell_1$ outputs let WJ be
    computed directly in nmslib's \texttt{WeightedJaccard} HNSW
    space~\cite{shape2vec}, no post-processing.
  \item A \textbf{diagnosis}: at scale, ranking objectives (triplet, InfoNCE)
    drop to random-projection level --- not for lack of capacity, but because
    recall needs WJ \emph{rank preservation}, not pairwise separation, and the loss
    damages the deployed layer (\S\ref{sec:exp}).
  \item \textbf{WJ-native distillation}: regressing the embedding's WJ onto the
    true WJ aligns objective with metric, giving a single
    Matryoshka~\cite{matryoshka22} encoder that beats random projection at near-
    \emph{and} wide-net recall at scale --- the first learned WJ embedding to do so
    (\S\ref{sec:exp}).
\end{enumerate}

%% ---------------- 2. Related Work ----------------
\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
ShapeToVec~\cite{shape2vec} produces the quadtree WJ vectors we compress.
WJ \emph{sketching} --- ICWS~\cite{ioffe10}, ProbMinHash~\cite{probminhash20},
PolyMinHash~\cite{polyminhash25} --- gives unbiased estimators but integer codes
incompatible with float-vector ANN indexes. Learned
hashing~\cite{hashsurvey14,deephashsurvey22} and Matryoshka representation
learning~\cite{matryoshka22} target Hamming/cosine spaces and degrade on the WJ
simplex (Table~\ref{tab:results}); NMF~\cite{nmf99} gives simplex factors but
optimises reconstruction, not retrieval. Knowledge distillation~\cite{hinton15}
transfers a teacher's outputs to a student; we adapt it to a \emph{metric},
distilling exact WJ into a compact simplex embedding --- none of the above target
WJ as the native metric.

%% ---------------- 3. Method ----------------
\section{Method}
Given ShapeToVec vectors $\mathbf{x}\in\Delta^{D}$ ($D{=}18{,}219$) and ground-truth
WJ neighbour pairs, we learn $f_\theta:\Delta^{D}\!\to\!\Delta^{d}$ ($d\le512$)
preserving WJ for fast HNSW retrieval (Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline}).

\subsection{WJ-native simplex embedding}
\label{sec:embed}
The encoder is a three-layer MLP ($18{,}219\!\to\!4096\!\to\!1024\!\to\!d$). An MLP
is deliberate: boundary Transformers~\cite{mai22}, vertex GNNs~\cite{polygongnn24}
and set encoders~\cite{deepsets17} aggregate \emph{local} context and assume
cosine/Euclidean outputs, whereas WJ is a \emph{global} statistic over all $D$
dimensions that is distorted when cosine features are forced onto the simplex; the
dense first layer mixes all dimensions globally (and holds ${\sim}75$M of the
${\sim}80$M parameters). Because quadtree vectors are extremely sparse
($\sim$73\% zeros), we place BatchNorm~\cite{ioffe15bn} \emph{before} each ReLU to
avoid a near-degenerate output. WJ requires non-negative inputs, enforced by a
simplex projection:
\begin{equation}
  f_\theta(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{\mathrm{ReLU}(h_\theta(\mathbf{x}))}
       {\max(\|\mathrm{ReLU}(h_\theta(\mathbf{x}))\|_1,\;\epsilon)} \in \Delta^{d},
  \label{eq:simplex}
\end{equation}
making $WJ(f_\theta(\mathbf{x}),f_\theta(\mathbf{y}))$ well-defined and directly
indexable in nmslib's \texttt{WeightedJaccard} space~\cite{shape2vec}.

\subsection{Contrastive objectives and why they break at scale}
\label{sec:contrastive}
Our first route is metric learning. With anchor--positive pairs and in-batch
cross-WJ $S_{ij}=WJ(f_\theta(\mathbf{a}_i),f_\theta(\mathbf{p}_j))$, a
\emph{false-negative filter} $\mathcal{G}$ masks a query's true neighbours before
mining the hardest negative $j^\star$, and a triplet loss~\cite{schroff15} with
margin $m$,
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{trip}}=\big[\,WJ(\mathbf{n}_{j^\star},\mathbf{a})
     -WJ(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a})+m\,\big]_{+},
  \label{eq:triplet}
\end{equation}
separates positives from negatives; a decoder reconstruction term and a
non-saturating InfoNCE~\cite{oord18},
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{NCE}}=-\log\frac{\exp(S_{ii}/\tau)}
       {\sum_{j:(i,j)\notin\mathcal{G}}\exp(S_{ij}/\tau)},
  \label{eq:infonce}
\end{equation}
extend the supervised neighbourhood. These are strong at 10k (Table~\ref{tab:results}).
\emph{Yet all are separation proxies}: they push positives above negatives, which
does \emph{not} guarantee the global WJ \emph{ranking} that recall depends on. At
the 187k scale (\S\ref{sec:exp}) their Stage-1 recall ties an untrained random
projection and \emph{flatlines} with $k$. Worse, the loss \emph{damages the layer
it is applied to}: reading the deployed output gives the \emph{worst} recall in the
network (R@500 $0.65$, below a random projection's $0.77$), while an untouched
intermediate layer is best ($0.82$, Fig.~\ref{fig:layer}) --- the optimised layer is
the wrong one to deploy. Rank fidelity collapses with it: the Spearman correlation
between embedding-WJ and true-WJ falls from $0.76$ at that intermediate layer to
$0.14$ at the deployed output, tracking the recall drop. This motivates supervising
the metric itself.

\subsection{WJ-native distillation}
\label{sec:distill}
We align the objective with the metric directly. For a batch of $M$ encodings we
form the true WJ on the original $D$-dim vectors, $T_{ij}=WJ(\mathbf{x}_i,\mathbf{x}_j)$,
and the embedding WJ, $\hat T_{ij}=WJ(f_\theta(\mathbf{x}_i),f_\theta(\mathbf{x}_j))$, and minimise
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{dist}}=\frac{1}{M^2}\sum_{i,j}\big(\hat T_{ij}-T_{ij}\big)^2 .
  \label{eq:distill}
\end{equation}
The objective \emph{is} the retrieval metric, so the deployed output is exactly the
optimised quantity --- a metric distillation~\cite{hinton15} with exact $D$-dim WJ
as teacher and the compact simplex embedding as student. We apply
Eq.~\eqref{eq:distill} on nested prefixes $\{256,\dots,4096\}$
(Matryoshka~\cite{matryoshka22}, each $\ell_1$-renormalised), so one model truncates
to any width; the smallest already dominates (\S\ref{sec:exp}).

\subsection{Two-stage retrieval}
Stage-1 builds an HNSW index ($M{=}20$, $\mathrm{ef}{=}200$)~\cite{hnsw20} in
nmslib's \texttt{WeightedJaccard} space~\cite{shape2vec} over the $d$-dim
embeddings, returning top-$K$; Stage-2 reranks by exact WJ on the original vectors
(Numba~\cite{lam15}). $K$ trades recall for speed.

%% ---------------- 4. Experiments ----------------
\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}
We use OSM Parks polygons~\cite{osm,shape2vec} at two scales: 10k (8k database / 2k
queries) and the full corpus (187{,}019 database; 46{,}754 queries). At 10k the 2k
queries are disjoint from the 8k database. At full scale, the distillation encoder is
trained only on corpus-internal neighbour pairs and evaluated on \emph{all}
46{,}754 queries --- none seen in training --- so the numbers measure generalization
to entirely unseen queries. Ground truth is exact top-500 WJ on the $D$-dim vectors. We report R@$k$ ($k\in\{10,50,500\}$) and QPS (PyTorch~\cite{paszke19},
AdamW~\cite{loshchilov19}, cosine annealing~\cite{loshchilov17}; single GPU).

\noindent\textbf{Moderate scale (Table~\ref{tab:results}).}
WJ-distillation is the strongest method: R@50 $=0.930$, $+7.2$\,pp over the best
contrastive model and above brute-force ICWS (0.897) at ${>}100\times$ its
throughput. Reranking lifts R@10 above $0.996$ for all variants.

\noindent\textbf{Full scale (Table~\ref{tab:full}).}
At $23\times$ the database, the contrastive advantage collapses: AE WJ-triplet+recon
ties random projection on R@50 and flatlines (R@500 $=$ R@50 $=0.656$); InfoNCE
recovers wide-net recall (R@500 0.727) but never wins both ends. WJ-distillation, by
aligning objective and metric, reaches Stage-1 R@500 $=0.890$ at 256-d ---
$+16$\,pp over both, on all 46{,}754 held-out queries --- and after reranking gives
the highest recall at the highest throughput: R@500 $=0.966$ vs.\ random
projection's $0.927$ at nearly $2\times$ the QPS. This is, to our knowledge, the
first learned WJ embedding to beat random projection on \emph{both} the near- and
wide-net frontiers at scale; recall no longer flatlines (R@500 $\gg$ R@50).

%% ---------------- 5. Conclusion ----------------
\section{Conclusion}
We compress ShapeToVec WJ vectors up to $71\times$ with a WJ-native simplex
embedding. Contrastive objectives suffice at moderate scale but fail at 187k ---
they optimise separation, not the WJ ranking recall needs, and damage the deployed
layer. WJ-native distillation removes the misalignment, and a single Matryoshka
encoder beats random projection at both near- and wide-net recall while running
faster --- the first learned WJ embedding to do so at scale. Future work targets
billion-scale corpora and other simplex-embeddable set metrics.
